02 / Ultralearning

Learn deeply on purpose

I am an ultralearner. I take responsibility for designing the way I learn instead of waiting for a class, project, or credential to do it for me. I choose a demanding target, identify the smallest set of fundamentals that supports it, and build direct practice around the real skill.

By deep learning, I do not mean machine learning. I mean understanding something beyond recognition: retrieving it without notes, applying it in a real system, finding where my mental model breaks, correcting it, and explaining the result clearly enough that someone else can follow it.

04 / Working system

Focus. Build. Test. Review.

I structure my days around repeatable standards instead of a public timetable. The point is to protect attention, turn study into evidence, and make improvement measurable.

  1. Choose
    Define the hard problem

    Pick one demanding target, write down what “done” means, and identify the fundamentals it depends on.

  2. Retrieve
    Work without leaning on notes

    Recall commands, concepts, and failure modes from memory; record the gaps instead of hiding them.

  3. Build
    Put the model into a real system

    Turn the concept into code, a lab, or an operational workflow where the assumptions can be tested.

  4. Train
    Protect sustained performance

    Physical training and deliberate recovery keep the learning pace consistent rather than temporary.

  5. Review
    Close the loop

    Document evidence, explain the result, update the backlog, and make the next attempt more precise.

The standard is simple: understand it, prove it, explain it, improve it.
